How to work out how many skeins of yarn a blanket needs

Blanket yardage swings sharply with stitch density, hook or needle size, and fiber elasticity. This guide shows how to scale accurately from a real swatch and how to sanity-check against standard blanket sizes.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Knit or crochet a swatch at least 4 × 4 in (10 × 10 cm) using the exact stitch pattern, yarn, and needle or hook size planned for the blanket.
  2. Wash, dry, or block the swatch the same way you will care for the finished blanket, then measure its relaxed width and height.
  3. Find how much yarn the swatch consumed by unraveling and measuring it, or by weighing the swatch on a gram scale and converting with the ball band weight-to-yardage ratio.
  4. Enter your target blanket dimensions, single-skein yardage from the label, number of strands held together, and a 10% to 15% allowance for borders and joins.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Finished blanket width: Target finished width (e.g. 50 in for a throw).
  • Finished blanket length: Target finished length (e.g. 60 in for a throw).
  • Measured swatch width: Measure after washing or blocking.
  • Measured swatch height: Measure after washing or blocking.
  • Yarn used in the swatch: Unravel and measure, or weigh and convert from the ball band.
  • Yarn length per skein: Printed on the yarn label in yards or meters.
  • Strands held together: Keep at 1 if swatch yarn already includes all strands.
  • Extra allowance: Typical buffer is 10% to 15% for borders and joins.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Relying solely on a generic yarn-weight chart. Crochet stitches often consume 25% to 30% more yarn than stockinette knitting of the same dimensions.
  • Leaving out border edging. A multi-round crochet border on a large blanket can easily take one to two extra skeins.
  • Comparing skeins instead of total yardage when substituting yarn brands. Two worsted-weight skeins can differ by more than 100 yards.
  • Buying skeins from different dye lots. Even subtle shade shifts become obvious across a wide flat blanket.

Practical ordering checklist

  • Record yarn used per single strand if you use the strands-held-together multiplier.
  • Add 15% to 20% extra when combining cables, bobbles, or textured stitches with a wide border.
  • Save one ball band with dye-lot and fiber details in case you need a repair skein later.
